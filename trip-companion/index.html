EZE strike watch

Postponed as of Sept 3

What it is. ATE-ANAC (state workers in the civil aviation authority) called a 48-hour action at 27+ airports including Ezeiza and Aeroparque, in two daily windows: 09:00–12:00 and 18:00–21:00 local. Only medical, humanitarian, organ-transport and official flights were guaranteed inside the windows. The government ordered 75% minimum service.

Where it stands. The Sept 3–4 action was postponed on the morning of Sept 3 as a "gesture of good faith" while the union presses the Labour Ministry for a hearing. The union says it may resume Sept 7–8 if wage talks fail. Nothing is scheduled for Sept 5, so your 09:45 arrival is outside any announced window.

If it does resume while you are in country: arriving international flights still land, but expect slower immigration, ground handling and baggage in the windows. Domestic legs on Aerolíneas are hit hardest. Plan any onward flight outside 09–12 and 18–21, and check the night before.

ATL connection

Do not exit security
  • Bags are checked through to EZE on one ticket. Do not go to baggage claim and do not leave the secure area. No second TSA screening.
  • Regional jets from VPS usually park at T, A, B or C. Delta to Buenos Aires has been leaving from Concourse E (E27/E28) or F. Confirm the gate in the app the moment you land; ATL swaps E↔F often.
  • Plane Train: board in the direction marked International Terminal / Concourse F. Stops are T-A-B-C-D-E-F. Count on 2 min per stop plus 5 min to walk to the platform.
  • E↔F on foot is about 10 min. F is short (a quarter mile). Sky Clubs: Concourse F mezzanine, Concourse E center, Concourse B.
  • International boarding opens ~50 min before departure and the door closes 15 min before. Gate agents check passports at the door, not at the desk; keep it in hand.
  • Delta app "Delta Sync" free Wi-Fi works on the A330/767; download meeting files before takeoff anyway.

Argentina: rules and money

Verified Sept 4, 2026
Immigration (Migraciones)
  • US passport: no visa, no fee for business or tourism up to 90 days. Passport must be valid on entry with one blank page; six months' validity is the safe standard.
  • Counter process: passport scan, fingerprints and photo, a question or two ("¿Motivo del viaje?" — "Negocios"). Have the hotel name and neighborhood ready. E-gates are for Argentine passports only.
  • Health insurance (Decree 366/2025): since July 2025 foreigners must hold travel medical cover including hospitalization, evacuation and repatriation; commonly cited minimum US$20,000. Enforcement at EZE has been inconsistent and a June 2026 court ruling clouded the decree, but officers can ask. Carry a screenshot of your policy or your credit card's travel-medical benefit letter.
  • Onward ticket: rarely requested at EZE. Your return e-ticket in the Delta app is enough.
  • No paper arrival card. No reciprocity fee (abolished 2016). No COVID forms.
Customs (Aduana / ARCA)
  • Every bag is X-rayed after claim. Random secondary checks happen.
  • Allowance by air: US$500 of goods bought abroad plus another US$500 from the arrivals duty-free. Excess taxed at 50%.
  • One laptop and one phone per person are exempt as personal items, outside the allowance. Work gear beyond that (second laptop, drones, pro camera) should be declared on the ARCA "Mi Equipaje" app before landing for the express lane.
  • Cash or instruments of US$10,000+ must be declared. No fresh fruit, meat, plants. Prescription meds in original packaging with the script.
Money
  • The old blue/official gap has closed: official ≈ 1,480 ARS, blue ≈ 1,500, MEP ≈ 1,500 per USD (early Sept 2026). Rates move; treat as ballpark.
  • Pay by Visa/Mastercard: foreign cards settle at the MEP "tourist" rate automatically. Use a no-foreign-fee card. Tap-to-pay is everywhere in Buenos Aires.
  • Skip ATMs (low caps, fees around US$10 per withdrawal). If you need pesos, bring crisp US$100 bills and change small amounts at a bank-owned "casa de cambio" or Western Union, never with street "arbolitos" at the airport.
  • Tipping: 10% in restaurants, cash preferred; a "cubierto" cover charge on the bill is not the tip. Round up taxis.
  • Receipts for business expenses: ask for "factura" (invoice) and they will print one.
Ground transport from EZE
  • Official remis/taxi counters (Taxi Ezeiza, Manuel Tienda León) are inside the arrivals hall, before the outside doors. Fixed voucher fare to Palermo/Recoleta/Microcentro roughly US$45–70 depending on the rate, tolls included; card accepted at the counter. ~35 km, 40–70 min.
  • Uber / Cabify / DiDi work; the signed app pickup point is outside near Meeting Point I. Rides are usually 20–35% cheaper than the counters; pay in-app. Never get into a car with someone who approached you inside the hall.
  • Tienda León bus to Terminal Madero (Retiro/Puerto Madero) about ARS 16,500, every 30 min; slowest but reliable.
  • In the city: taxis are black-and-yellow with a meter; Cabify/Uber are legal and cheap; Subte line D serves Palermo–Recoleta–downtown. Load a SUBE card for buses/subte or tap a contactless card.
Safety and emergencies
  • US State Dept advisory for Argentina is Level 1 (lowest). The real risk is phone snatching: motochorros grab phones from hands at curbs and café tables. Keep it pocketed at the airport curb and on sidewalks; use it inside the car.
  • Keep your passport in the hotel safe; a photo of it plus a driver's license is enough on the street. Demonstrations gather at Plaza de Mayo and Congreso, usually announced a day ahead.
  • Emergency: 911. Tourist police: 0800-999-5000. US Embassy Buenos Aires: Av. Colombia 4300, Palermo, +54 11 5777-4533 (24 h for citizens).
  • Tap water is fine in Buenos Aires. Private clinics (Hospital Alemán, Hospital Italiano, Swiss Medical) are excellent and take travel insurance.
Business etiquette, connectivity, practicalities
  • Meetings: a light cheek-kiss greeting is normal even in business, one kiss, right cheek. Small talk first. Lunch 13:00–15:00, dinner from 21:00. Punctuality is looser for social plans, not for meetings.
  • WhatsApp is the business channel; ask for a WhatsApp number instead of email for anything time-sensitive.
  • Power: 220 V / 50 Hz, Type I (angled flat pins) sockets, Type C in older buildings. Bring the adapter; your laptop brick handles the voltage.
  • Data: an eSIM (Airalo, Holafly) activated before landing, or a Claro/Personal SIM in the arrivals hall. EZE Wi-Fi is free.
  • Weather Sept 5: early spring, roughly 36–55 °F with wind. A light coat over business dress.
  • Time: Argentina stays on UTC−3 year-round. Your 9 AM Central call is 12:00 in Buenos Aires.
